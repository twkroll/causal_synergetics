\title{Testing Synergetic Macrostates for Intervention Sufficiency: Controlled Projectability, Frozen Minimal Witnesses, and Output-Preserving Preparation}

\author{AUTHOR INPUT REQUIRED}
\affiliation{AUTHOR INPUT REQUIRED}
\email{AUTHOR INPUT REQUIRED}

\begin{abstract}
Classical synergetic reduction asks whether a small set of order parameters describes dynamics whose remaining degrees of freedom are slaved or rapidly relaxing. Such passive or present-state sufficiency need not determine response to a declared intervention family. For the full retained-trajectory response used here, exact fibre homogeneity is the standard controlled-projectability/closure condition; we do not claim that criterion as new, but use it diagnostically for a pre-declared slaving/order-parameter map and express finite-horizon deviations with standard fast-slow error ingredients. Prospectively frozen minimal witnesses then delimit the diagnostic. Function-equivalent linear and two-unit ReLU parameterisations exhibit different one-step learning responses, while learned-coordinate pilots supply negative evidence: the response coordinate is WEAK against equal-dimensional raw PCA, a nuisance-invariance gate ends in a specification-classification FAIL, and an exact symmetry-aware Gram representation matches it. In a symmetric two-machine swing benchmark, a representative coherent macro is passively exact but loses accuracy under a localized disturbance, whereas the arithmetic mean/center-of-inertia coordinate remains exactly closed. A bounded output-preserving preparation then moves the hidden coherency state to the known forced relative equilibrium; after preparation is removed, the matched condition follows the coherent aggregate and outperforms no preparation and an equal-cost sign-mismatched condition. We claim neither a new quotient criterion, a universal cross-domain law, nor a generic preparation/control method; the contribution is a restricted synergetics-centered diagnostic synthesis of established criteria, frozen witnesses, countercontrols, and one preparation benchmark.
\end{abstract}
